Boston & the Trail

Everything on this page is a short walk from our door.

We are not a guidebook. But we do give directions all day, so here is the version we give at the counter.

  1. Home
  2. Boston

Next door

Faneuil Hall

Built in 1742 as a market and a meeting hall, and used for both ever since. It is stop nine of sixteen on the Freedom Trail, and the grasshopper weathervane on the cupola is the oldest thing in the neighbourhood that still does its job.

Two minutes from our door, less if you cut through the canopy.

Behind us

Quincy Market

The long granite hall with the dome. Inside is a corridor of food stalls; outside, the cobbled square where the street performers work from 11am.

Stand under the rotunda and read the gold lettering around it. It has been making the same claim about serving the people of Boston since 1826.

The red line

The Freedom Trail

Two and a half miles, sixteen sites, one red line set into the pavement. It runs past the marketplace, which is why half the people who come through our door are already halfway round it.

Walk it north and you end up in the North End; walk it south and you hit the Old State House in about four minutes.

Five minutes north

The North End

Boston’s oldest residential neighbourhood, and the one people plan their evening around. Narrow streets, a pastry queue that does not shorten, and Paul Revere’s house at the far end.

From the marketplace it is one crossing of the greenway and you are there.

Five minutes east

The Harbour and Long Wharf

Long Wharf, the ferries, the aquarium and the water. On a clear afternoon it is the best ten-minute detour in the city, and it explains why half the shop is cod, lobster and ships.

Worth the walk

Beacon Hill and the Common

Fifteen minutes west, past the Old State House and up the hill: gas lamps, brick sidewalks and Acorn Street, which is the most photographed lane in the city for good reason.

Boston Common and the Public Garden sit at the bottom of it, and the Trail starts there.

Getting around

Three stations, all close.

State Orange and Blue lines. Comes out beside the Old State House, about three minutes away.
Haymarket Orange and Green lines, plus the bus. Four minutes, on the north side of the marketplace.
Government Center Green and Blue lines. Five minutes across the plaza.
On foot The whole marketplace is pedestrian. There is no driving up to the door.

And then

Come back before nine.

Whatever else the afternoon turns into, the shop is open until nine Monday to Saturday and seven on Sunday. The staircase is in the middle.
